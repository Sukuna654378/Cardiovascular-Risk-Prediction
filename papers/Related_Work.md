# Related Work Section Draft

## 1. Scope & Domain Overview
The application of machine learning (ML) to 10-year coronary heart disease (CHD) risk prediction has expanded rapidly as researchers seek to improve upon traditional multivariable clinical calculators such as the Framingham Risk Score (FRS) ([D'Agostino et al., 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2748237/); [Krittanawong et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5538302/)). While classical risk calculators rely on linear proportional hazards models, contemporary data science approaches leverage non-linear ensemble architectures to capture non-additive physiological interactions ([Raman et al., 2026](https://www.ispor.org/heor-resources/presentations-database/presentation-cti/ispor-2026/poster-session-1-4/machine-learning-based-prediction-of-10-year-coronary-heart-disease-risk-and-comparison-with-the-framingham-risk-score); [Tasnim et al., 2025](https://etasr.com/index.php/ETASR/article/view/9152)). However, translating these algorithmic models into trustworthy clinical decision support tools requires rigorous attention to pipeline integrity, metric selection under class imbalance, probability calibration, and sex-stratified fairness ([Kasartzian & Tsiampalis, 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-1729/15/1/94); [Whalen et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8117765/)).

## 2. Synthesis by Theme

### Theme 1: Non-Linear Machine Learning vs. Invasive High-Dimensional Features
Recent evaluations on the Framingham cohort show that tree ensembles (XGBoost, Random Forest, HistGradientBoosting) achieve competitive discrimination scores (ROC-AUC 0.70–0.73) compared to static risk charts ([Raman et al., 2026](https://www.ispor.org/heor-resources/presentations-database/presentation-cti/ispor-2026/poster-session-1-4/machine-learning-based-prediction-of-10-year-coronary-heart-disease-risk-and-comparison-with-the-framingham-risk-score); [MDPI Applied Sciences, 2025](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394361214_Machine-Learning_Insights_from_the_Framingham_Heart_Study_Enhancing_Cardiovascular_Risk_Prediction_and_Monitoring)). In large population cohorts, [Weng et al. (2017)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0174944) demonstrated a +3.6% improvement in event prediction over standard ACC/AHA guidelines ([Arnett et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7734661/)), while automated ML frameworks like AutoPrognosis achieved an ROC-AUC of 0.774 across UK Biobank participants ([Alaa et al., 2019](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0213653)). However, these high-performing pipelines rely heavily on extensive blood biomarkers or hundreds of clinical variables. Whether non-linear algorithms maintain superiority when restricted exclusively to routine, non-invasive primary care markers remains an open question ([Lloyd-Jones et al., 2006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2748238/); [Siontis et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3384218/)).

### Theme 2: Pipeline Leakage and Methodological Integrity in Tabular Benchmarks
A critical vulnerability in clinical machine learning literature is data leakage during data preparation ([Kapoor & Narayanan, 2023](https://linkinghub.elsevier.com/retrieve/pii/S2666389923001599)). Preprocessing transformations—such as global feature scaling, missing value imputation ($k$-NN), or synthetic oversampling—are routinely applied across the entire dataset prior to cross-validation splitting ([Tasnim et al., 2025](https://etasr.com/index.php/ETASR/article/view/9152)). This introduces subtle test-set contamination, giving models unfair access to global distribution parameters and yielding overly optimistic performance estimates ([Kasartzian & Tsiampalis, 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-1729/15/1/94)). Strict pipeline encapsulation—where all imputations and transformations are fit purely within training folds—is essential to establish true clinical generalizability.

### Theme 3: Class Imbalance, Calibration Deficit, and Clinical Net Benefit
Epidemiological datasets present steep class imbalance; the Framingham cohort exhibits a 10-year CHD incidence rate of approximately 15.1% ([D'Agostino et al., 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2748237/)). Evaluating models via standard accuracy (63%–86%) or ROC-AUC masks severe sensitivity collapse on high-risk patients ([Adhikari et al., 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.07.25321756v1)). Because ROC-AUC is insensitive to class skew, classifiers can report high AUC scores while missing up to 90% of true positive cases at default 0.50 decision thresholds ([Saito & Rehmsmeier, 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0118432)). Furthermore, complex tree ensembles frequently generate uncalibrated, extreme probability outputs near decision boundaries ([Chen & Guestrin, 2016](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.02754); [Van Calster et al., 2016](https://www.jclinepi.com/article/S0895-4356(15)00581-8/abstract)). Post-hoc recalibration and Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) across actionable threshold ranges (10%–30%) are required to confirm clinical net benefit ([Vickers & Elkin, 2006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2577036/); [Lundberg & Lee, 2017](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07874)).

### Theme 4: Sex Heterogeneity and Algorithmic Bias
Cardiovascular risk factor dynamics differ substantially between biological sexes. Traditional equation-based risk tools have historically overestimated absolute risk in low-incidence female cohorts ([D'Agostino et al., 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2748237/); [Lloyd-Jones et al., 2006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2748238/)). Although recent ML studies include biological sex as a static predictive feature ([Raman et al., 2026](https://www.ispor.org/heor-resources/presentations-database/presentation-cti/ispor-2026/poster-session-1-4/machine-learning-based-prediction-of-10-year-coronary-heart-disease-risk-and-comparison-with-the-framingham-risk-score); [MDPI Applied Sciences, 2025](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394361214_Machine-Learning_Insights_from_the_Framingham_Heart_Study_Enhancing_Cardiovascular_Risk_Prediction_and_Monitoring)), few explicitly assess sex-stratified calibration curves or subgroup-specific decision utility ([Whalen et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8117765/)). Evaluating subgroup calibration is necessary to prevent machine learning algorithms from propagating historical clinical disparities.

## 3. Positioning & Research Contribution (The Gap)
Existing studies on 10-year cardiovascular risk prediction frequently report high apparent discrimination using complex tree ensembles or automated machine learning pipelines ([Raman et al., 2026](https://www.ispor.org/heor-resources/presentations-database/presentation-cti/ispor-2026/poster-session-1-4/machine-learning-based-prediction-of-10-year-coronary-heart-disease-risk-and-comparison-with-the-framingham-risk-score); [Adhikari et al., 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.07.25321756v1); [MDPI Applied Sciences, 2025](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394361214_Machine-Learning_Insights_from_the_Framingham_Heart_Study_Enhancing_Cardiovascular_Risk_Prediction_and_Monitoring); [Tasnim et al., 2025](https://etasr.com/index.php/ETASR/article/view/9152); [Weng et al., 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0174944)). However, these benchmarks routinely suffer from subtle preprocessing data leakage, ignore probability miscalibration near decision boundaries, and fail to optimize decision thresholds for minority class sensitivity ([Saito & Rehmsmeier, 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0118432); [Van Calster et al., 2016](https://www.jclinepi.com/article/S0895-4356(15)00581-8/abstract); [Kapoor & Narayanan, 2023](https://linkinghub.elsevier.com/retrieve/pii/S2666389923001599)). To our knowledge, no prior work has systematically evaluated ElasticNet Logistic Regression against non-linear tree ensembles under a strictly leak-free pipeline with joint Precision-Recall threshold optimization, Brier calibration analysis, and sex-stratified Decision Curve Analysis using routine non-invasive clinical interaction markers on the 3,390-patient Framingham 10-year CHD dataset.
