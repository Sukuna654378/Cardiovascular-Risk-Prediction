# Thematic Map & Research Gap Formulation

---

## 1. Thematic Map (Idea-Driven Categorization)

### Theme 1: Complex non-linear models demonstrate moderate discrimination gains in cardiovascular prediction but frequently require high-dimensional or invasive laboratory markers.
Existing cardiovascular machine learning studies focus heavily on maximizing discrimination metrics through complex ensemble models (XGBoost, Random Forest, Neural Networks) or automated ML pipelines. However, these models often rely on hundreds of variables or invasive lab results unavailable in routine primary care screening.
* **Key References:** [Raman et al. (2026)](https://www.ispor.org/heor-resources/presentations-database/presentation-cti/ispor-2026/poster-session-1-4/machine-learning-based-prediction-of-10-year-coronary-heart-disease-risk-and-comparison-with-the-framingham-risk-score), [MDPI Applied Sciences (2025)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394361214_Machine-Learning_Insights_from_the_Framingham_Heart_Study_Enhancing_Cardiovascular_Risk_Prediction_and_Monitoring), [Tasnim et al. (2025)](https://etasr.com/index.php/ETASR/article/view/9152), [Weng et al. (2017)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0174944), [Alaa et al. (2019)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0213653).

### Theme 2: Methodological data leakage during preprocessing and feature engineering frequently leads to artificially inflated clinical benchmark results
A prevalent issue across published tabular clinical ML benchmarks is improper pipeline encapsulation. Executing scaling, oversampling, or $k$-NN missing data imputation globally across the entire dataset prior to cross-validation splits causes test-fold data contamination and overly optimistic performance estimates
* **Key References:** [Kapoor & Narayanan (2023)](https://linkinghub.elsevier.com/retrieve/pii/S2666389923001599), [Kasartzian & Tsiampalis (2025)](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-1729/15/1/94), [Whalen et al. (2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8117765/).

### Theme 3: Evaluating cardiovascular risk models using standard accuracy and ROC-AUC under severe class imbalance masks critical sensitivity collapse and probability miscalibration.
Epidemiological cohorts typically exhibit a low positive event rate (~15%). Relying on overall accuracy creates an "accuracy paradox," while standard ROC-AUC remains overly optimistic. Furthermore, tree-based ensembles generate uncalibrated probability scores near boundary thresholds, missing up to 90% of true high-risk cases when using default 0.50 decision cutoffs.
* **Key References:** [Saito & Rehmsmeier (2015)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0118432), [Adhikari et al. (2025)](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.07.25321756v1), [Van Calster et al. (2016)](https://www.jclinepi.com/article/S0895-4356(15)00581-8/abstract), [Vickers & Elkin (2006)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2577036/).

### Theme 4: Traditional clinical calculators and modern machine learning models exhibit systematic calibration disparities across biological sexes.
Cardiovascular risk factors manifest differently between male and female cohorts. Classical risk tools (e.g., FRS) historically overestimate absolute risk in low-incidence female subgroups, yet modern ML literature rarely evaluates sex-stratified calibration slopes or subgroup Decision Curve Analysis (DCA).
* **Key References:** [D'Agostino et al. (2008)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2748237/), [Lloyd-Jones et al. (2006)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2748238/), [Arnett et al. (2019)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7734661/), [Whalen et al. (2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8117765/).

## 2. Research Gap Formulation (Introduction & Related Work Pivot Sentence)

> **"Existing works** train standard machine learning models on cardiovascular risk datasets using global imputation, unengineered raw features, naive accuracy evaluation, and default 0.50 classification decision thresholds ([Raman et al., 2026](https://www.ispor.org/heor-resources/presentations-database/presentation-cti/ispor-2026/poster-session-1-4/machine-learning-based-prediction-of-10-year-coronary-heart-disease-risk-and-comparison-with-the-framingham-risk-score); [Adhikari et al., 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.07.25321756v1); [MDPI Applied Sciences, 2025](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394361214_Machine-Learning_Insights_from_the_Framingham_Heart_Study_Enhancing_Cardiovascular_Risk_Prediction_and_Monitoring); [Tasnim et al., 2025](https://etasr.com/index.php/ETASR/article/view/9152); [Weng et al., 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0174944)). **However,** these studies routinely suffer from subtle preprocessing data leakage, ignore probability miscalibration near decision boundaries, and fail to optimize decision thresholds for minority class sensitivity ([Saito & Rehmsmeier, 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0118432); [Van Calster et al., 2016](https://www.jclinepi.com/article/S0895-4356(15)00581-8/abstract); [Kapoor & Narayanan, 2023](https://linkinghub.elsevier.com/retrieve/pii/S2666389923001599)). **To our knowledge, no prior work has** systematically evaluated ElasticNet Logistic Regression against non-linear tree ensembles under a strictly leak-free pipeline with joint Precision-Recall threshold optimization, Brier calibration analysis, and sex-stratified Decision Curve Analysis using routine non-invasive clinical interaction markers **on the 3,390-patient Framingham 10-year CHD dataset.**"
